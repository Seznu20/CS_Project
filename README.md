This project investigates the vulnerability of deep learning–based medical image classifiers to adversarial attacks, and evaluates practical defense strategies to mitigate that vulnerability. A convolutional neural network (MedCNN) was built and trained from scratch in PyTorch to classify chest X-ray images into two categories, Normal and Pneumonia. Once the classifier achieved strong clean-data performance, it was subjected to four distinct categories of adversarial attack the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), DeepFool, and a localized Adversarial Patch attack all implemented from first principles without relying on existing adversarial-ML libraries such as Foolbox or ART.
The central finding is that even a well-trained, high-accuracy medical imaging model is highly susceptible to small, often visually imperceptible pixel-level perturbations. Accuracy on adversarially perturbed inputs dropped substantially relative to clean-data accuracy, with iterative attacks (PGD) proving considerably more damaging than single-step attacks (FGSM) at the same perturbation budget. Three defense mechanisms input smoothing, feature squeezing, and adversarial training were then implemented and benchmarked; adversarial training was found to be the most effective, recovering the largest share of lost accuracy, though at additional training cost.
